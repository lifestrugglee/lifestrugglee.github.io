About

I develop privacy-aware language-model methods that extract clinical information from electronic health records. In dental informatics, this work has shown that models trained on synthetic notes transfer across institutions, and that structured diagnoses tend to understate disease stage compared with the narrative notes.

My dissertation, advised by Dr. Xiaoqian Jiang at UTHealth Houston, reduces the annotation, prompt-engineering, and privacy barriers to clinical information extraction with large language models. In 2025 I spent a summer at PAII Inc. working on a 32B medical vision-language model.

Before informatics I trained as a respiratory therapist in Taiwan, worked as a software engineer, and studied cardiorespiratory physiology in a bioengineering master's. Clinical training taught me what a note is for; engineering taught me to build the tools that make a model useful outside a notebook.

Research direction

I want to turn fragmented clinical data into a reliable estimate of a patient's state, and to model how that state changes over time.

By patient state I mean the clinically meaningful phenotypes present at a given time, together with the model's confidence and the evidence behind each estimate. Notes, structured records, images, and physiological signals each capture part of that state, and none captures it completely. My plan has three connected parts, each building on the one before.

  1. Patient state from clinical narrative

    Extend locally deployable language models from entity extraction to time-indexed phenotypes, each with calibrated confidence and a link to the note text that supports it, and send uncertain or conflicting cases to clinicians for review.

  2. Disease trajectories from imperfect records

    Estimate how reliable each source is, from structured diagnoses to note-derived phenotypes and imaging, and carry that uncertainty into predictions of disease progression, starting with progression from prediabetes to type 2 diabetes.

  3. Multimodal disease state and treatment response

    Add overnight physiology to predict how older adults respond to treatment, beginning with positive airway pressure therapy for obstructive sleep apnea, and report performance across age, frailty, and demographic groups.

The long-term aim is a patient-level digital twin: a model that updates with each new note, measurement, and image, and forecasts outcomes that clinicians can check.

Selected work

  • Self-prompting small language models for clinical extraction

    Preprint, arXiv 2026, under review

    Small language models write and refine their own entity-specific prompts, and their self-prompt scores help screen which model suits a task. QLoRA fine-tuning followed by direct preference optimization on 200 annotated dental notes raised Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct from 0.780 micro-F1 with prompting alone to 0.864 on a 1,000-note held-out gold standard. The models run locally, so notes stay inside the institution.

    Read the preprint
    0.864micro-F1 across 19 entity types, up from 0.780

  • Synthetic notes that transfer across institutions

    JAMIA Open, 2025

    Replaced manually annotated training notes with 450 GPT-4 synthetic notes to train a spaCy/RoBERTa periodontal diagnosis extractor. On 360 expert-annotated notes per site, mean weighted F1 across five diagnostic entities was 0.96 at the development site and 0.95 at an external dental school. I coordinated the gold-standard annotation with three dental clinicians.

    Read the paper
    0.95weighted F1 at an external dental school

  • Structured diagnoses understate disease stage

    Manuscript under review

    Compared structured and narrative periodontal diagnoses across 4,542 dental encounters using SQL, transformer-based extraction, and periodontist adjudication. Each format matched the expert diagnosis only about half the time. Where both were recorded they disagreed in 14.8% of encounters, 8.4% by two or more stages.

    67.9%of disagreements had the milder stage in the structured field

  • Privacy risks in LLM-generated clinical notes

    JAMIA, 2025

    Designed PHI-injection and leakage-analysis methods to measure the privacy and utility trade-offs of synthetic clinical text, and compared conditioning and synthetic-PHI strategies through downstream ICD-9 coding. Related co-authored work compares de-identified, synthetic, and hybrid notes as ways to share clinical text.

  • Struggle Annotator

    Open-source tool, sole developer, Python and Streamlit

    A span-labeling and adjudication app that replaced our Excel-based workflow and the manual editing errors that came with it. Six dentists have used it across two projects of 720 and 1,200 notes.

  • Medical vision-language model

    Research Scientist Intern, PAII Inc., 2025

    Within a team project, fine-tuned a 32B vision-language model with LoRA on medical image-dialogue data, improving diagnosis extraction over the zero-shot baseline (results confidential). Built pipelines for imaging and multi-turn clinical conversations with structured prompting for anatomical localization and bounding boxes, and served the model with vLLM across four A100 GPUs.

    ~4×less weight memory with 4-bit GPTQ than BF16
